I have spent 9 years doing a version of this job without the title: more than 190 projects for clients across industries, usually starting with "here is our process, here is where it hurts", and ending with software in production that removes the pain. In 2027 I want to do it under its real name, **Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE)**. This post explains why, with the numbers that convinced me.

The data comes from three places: the public sources listed at the end, checked on 26 November 2026; job postings from OpenAI, Anthropic, Scale AI and Palantir; and **1,043 FDE postings collected by my own job-lead pipeline**, which my AI Jobs MCP server makes searchable ([the project page](/portfolio/ai-jobs-mcp-server-lambda-oauth/)). That pipeline crawls company career boards I chose, leans towards Europe and can list one job from several boards, so treat it as a large sample, not a census.

## What is a Forward Deployed Engineer?

An FDE is a software engineer whose office is the customer's problem. You work with the customer's team, often on site, to find the workflow that loses time or money, and you build and deploy software into their systems until it fixes that workflow. The output is a working system and a measured result, not a demo or a slide deck.

Palantir created the role in the early 2010s and called the team "Delta"; until about 2016 it had more FDEs than regular software engineers. AI labs adopted it because a strong model alone does not change a business: someone has to connect it to the data, the permissions, the edge cases and the people. OpenAI started its FDE team at the beginning of 2025 with 2 engineers and grew it to more than 10 across 8 cities on 3 continents. Salesforce tripled its FDE team in six months, and 40 to 50% of the people who moved into it came from inside the company.

| | Product engineer | Forward Deployed Engineer | Consultant |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Works on | One product, many users | One customer's problem, then the next | Advice and slides |
| Writes production code | Yes | Yes, in the customer's systems | Rarely |
| Talks to customers | Sometimes | Every day | Every day |
| Success measured by | Product metrics | The customer's business result | Client satisfaction |
| Travel | Little | 25% to 50% is common | Often |

## Why is FDE my dream job for 2027?

Four reasons, in the order they matter to me:

1. **A new problem every engagement.** Insurance claims one quarter, audit reports the next, a call center after that. My portfolio already looks like this: an [IFRS audit-review model](/portfolio/auditpro-ifrs-audit-review-llm/) that cut audit time from 44.0 s to 23.5 s, a [multi-tenant AI voice call center](/portfolio/nexuscall-ai-voice-call-center-crm/), a [private AI gateway](/portfolio/private-ai-gateway-litellm-openbao/) for company-wide model access.
2. **Real business results, not features.** An FDE is judged on whether the customer's process got faster, cheaper or safer. That is the measure I already use for my case studies.
3. **Travel and people.** Sitting with the team that does the work is how you find the real problem. I like that part of the job, and a role that pays you to do it is rare.
4. **The whole stack is in scope.** Data, back end, LLM inference, security, front end and deployment in one person or a small team. That is the full-stack AI work I like most.

## How fast is FDE demand growing?

Very fast, but the exact figure depends on who counts. Every source I found shows growth in the hundreds or thousands of percent, while software postings overall grew in the low teens.

Why the spread: Paraform counts roles on its own hiring platform, Lightcast and Indeed aggregate job boards, and Bloomberry analysed 1,000 postings collected by Revealera. They also compare different months. The direction is the same in all of them: FDE is one of the fastest-growing engineering titles in 2025 and 2026. Paraform adds that 83% of FDE roles are in San Francisco or New York.

## Who is hiring FDEs right now, and where?

My pipeline held **1,043 postings** with Forward Deployed or Deployment Strategist in the title on 26 November 2026. The companies with the most were Wonderful (54), Databricks (38), Palantir (34), OpenAI (32), Mistral AI (18), Cognition (12), Cohere (10) and Notion (10). Most titles are plain "Forward Deployed Engineer" (646), followed by Forward Deployed AI or ML Engineer (139), Deployment Strategist (92) and Forward Deployed Software Engineer (58). About half of the postings ask for a senior engineer.

## How much does a Forward Deployed Engineer earn?

More than a typical software engineer. Lightcast puts the median advertised FDE salary at over **$188,000**, against about **$145,000** for traditional software engineers. Levels.fyi lists a median total pay of **$225,000** from 164 US salaries, with the 25th percentile at $170K and the 75th at $300K.

## What skills does an FDE job ask for?

Engineering depth plus the ability to run a customer conversation. Bloomberry's analysis of 1,000 postings found Python in 66%, TypeScript in 35%, AI agents in 35%, AWS in 32%, LLM experience in 31% and RAG in 12%.

| Skill area | What postings ask for |
| --- | --- |
| Languages | Python first, TypeScript for full-stack, SQL (OpenAI asks for Postgres or MySQL) |
| LLM work | LLM APIs, prompting, agents and sub-agents, evals and guardrails, retrieval and vector databases |
| Protocols | MCP servers and agent skills (Anthropic, Scale AI) |
| Cloud | AWS, Azure or GCP, deploying into the customer's own infrastructure |
| Customer side | Scoping, statements of work, demos, explaining trade-offs to non-engineers |
| Experience | OpenAI: 7+ years full-stack. Anthropic: 3 to 4+ years in a customer-facing technical role |

My own pipeline tells the same story from 991 postings with a full description: integrations and APIs appear in 64%, customer-facing wording in 64%, AI agents in 62%, Python in 54%, LLMs in 45%, TypeScript or JavaScript in 29%, a cloud platform in 29%, RAG in 17%, evals in 15% and MCP in 13%. About 43% mention travel, most often 50%, 25% or 20% of the time.

If you want to prepare, the fastest path is to build two or three systems that solve a business problem end to end, measure the result, and write them up the way a customer would read them. That is also how I write my case studies.

## What are the downsides of being an FDE?

The honest list, from engineers and recruiters who have done the job:

- **Travel.** Up to 50% at OpenAI and 25 to 75% in a Palantir tactical role. That is a lot if you have a family or a strong local life.
- **Context switching and ambiguity.** New customer, new data, new politics every few months. Some FDEs describe it as two jobs at once.
- **Distance from the product.** At many companies FDE work does not flow back into the platform, which can slow promotion where there are few FDEs.
- **The label is getting diluted.** a16z warns of "Accenture for X", where pre-sales or overloaded generalists get the title. A recruiter quoted by The Pragmatic Engineer estimated that only about 10% of engineers want the role.

For me these are the reasons to want it. I would rather solve a new problem every quarter than polish one feature for three years. If you feel the opposite, a product engineering role is the better fit, and that is a perfectly good choice.

## How can a company work with a forward-deployed AI engineer today?

You do not need to hire a full FDE team to get the model. The useful part is the way of working: an engineer sits with your team, finds the workflow with the clearest cost, builds a working system into your stack, and measures the before and after. That is how I run engagements now, alone or with my team of full-stack, backend and DevOps engineers when the project needs more hands. The [portfolio](/portfolio/) shows what those engagements produced.
