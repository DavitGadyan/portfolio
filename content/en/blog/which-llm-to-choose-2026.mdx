A client asked me last month which model their new assistant should call: GPT, Claude, Gemini, or "one of the open ones". The honest answer is a table, not a name. Every family on the market is optimised for a different workload, and the cheapest way to build a good system is to understand which model to use for which job, how to combine them, and when self-hosting beats an API.

This post is the comparison I wish I had on that call. It covers the nine families that matter in November 2026: OpenAI GPT, Anthropic Claude, Google Gemini, xAI Grok, Meta (Llama and now Muse), Mistral, Alibaba Qwen, DeepSeek, and Microsoft Decision-1, a new kind of model that deserves [its own post](/blog/microsoft-decision-1-decision-model-use-cases/). It continues the series that started with [Snowflake vs Databricks](/blog/snowflake-vs-databricks-use-cases/) and [Azure, Fabric, Databricks or Foundry](/blog/azure-fabric-databricks-foundry-which-to-choose/), and it ends with the routing code I actually use.

## The short answer

Pick by job, not by brand. For general agents and tool calling, start with GPT or Claude. For software engineering agents, Claude first. For images, video and very large document sets, Gemini. For real-time social and market signals, Grok. For anything that must stay on your own hardware, Qwen, DeepSeek, Llama or Mistral on vLLM. For classification, routing and grading at volume, Microsoft Decision-1. Then benchmark two candidates on your own data, because every claim below is a vendor's claim until your evaluation set says otherwise.

Everything here was checked on 6 November 2026 against the vendors' model pages and the AI Board research report of 26 October 2026, which lists a vendor source per line. Prices are public list prices per million tokens, input then output, excluding volume discounts and batch rates.

## Why is there no single best LLM?

Because the families are optimised for different things. Some lead at complex reasoning and mathematics; some at reading and editing large codebases; some at video, audio and million-token documents; some at being cheap and fast for high-volume production work; and some publish their weights so you can run them on a GPU you control. A model that tops one of those lists is rarely first on the others, and the price spread between the top and the mid tier is five to ten times.

There are three kinds of provider, and the distinction matters more than any benchmark:

| Category | Providers | What you get |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Proprietary, commercial API | OpenAI, Google, Anthropic, xAI, Microsoft | Managed inference, the strongest models, tool calling, no hardware to run |
| Open-weight | Alibaba Qwen, DeepSeek, Meta, Mistral, OpenAI gpt-oss, NVIDIA Nemotron | Weights you download and serve yourself; customisation and data control |
| Enterprise platforms | Microsoft Foundry, Amazon Bedrock, Gemini Enterprise Agent Platform | One contract and one identity system for several vendors' models, plus evaluation and governance |

Open-weight does not mean open-source. Qwen and DeepSeek publish weights, but the licence, the training data and the commercial-use conditions differ per release, and some families publish only their smaller models.

## Proprietary API or self-hosted open weights?

This is the biggest architectural decision, bigger than the choice of model. Figure 1 shows both routes and the multi-model stack that combines them; tap a layer to read what it does.

<Stacks id="llm-routes" caption="Figure 1. Three ways to put a model behind an application: a proprietary API, self-hosted open weights, and the multi-model routing stack that uses both. The application and the orchestration layers are identical; only the base URL changes." />

| Question | Proprietary API | Self-hosted open weights |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hardware | None of yours; the vendor runs the GPUs | NVIDIA GPUs you rent or own, sized by you |
| Cost model | Per token; flagships $2 to $10 per million input | GPU hours plus operations; no per-token bill from a vendor |
| Model quality | The strongest models | A generation behind at the top, equal in the mid tier (Qwen3.8-27B, DeepSeek-V4-Flash, Mistral Medium 3.5) |
| Data path | Leaves your network under a data processing agreement; zero retention and regional processing are contract settings | Never leaves your VPC; embeddings run locally too |
| Customisation | Prompting, structured outputs, limited fine-tuning | Full fine-tuning (LoRA, QLoRA), custom serving, quantisation |
| Operations | Rate limits and provider availability are theirs | Scaling, batching, monitoring, patching and upgrades are yours |
| Switching cost | Low behind an abstraction layer | Low too: vLLM and SGLang expose OpenAI-compatible endpoints |

### What can you run on a 16 GB GPU?

A rule of thumb for a laptop with 16 GB of GPU memory, before production sizing. Memory depends on quantisation, context length, KV cache and architecture, so treat these as orders of magnitude.

| Model size | On a 16 GB GPU |
| --- | --- |
| 1B to 3B | Very comfortable, full precision |
| 7B to 8B | Practical at 16-bit, roomy at 8-bit |
| 14B | Practical with 4-bit quantisation and a modest context |
| 32B | Significant quantisation or CPU offloading compromises |
| 70B and above | Not practical in GPU memory; use a server or an API |

For production you would not use a laptop anyway: vLLM or SGLang on dedicated GPU instances, with batching and the KV cache tuned for your context lengths.

## Dense, mixture of experts, reasoning or decision model?

Not every model works the same way, and the architecture decides memory, latency and what the model is good for. Figure 2 puts the four kinds side by side.

<Figure src="llm-model-types.en.svg" caption="Figure 2. Four kinds of model. A dense model activates every parameter per token; a mixture of experts routes each token to a few experts; a reasoning model works through the problem before answering; a decision model scores fixed options with calibrated probabilities and writes no text." />

| Architecture | What happens per token | Memory and latency | Best application |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Dense | Every parameter participates; a 27B model computes 27B weights per token | Memory equals the parameter count; latency grows with size | General inference, simple serving, fine-tuning (Qwen3.8-27B, Mistral Medium 3.5) |
| Mixture of experts (MoE) | A router activates a few experts; DeepSeek-V4-Pro has 1.6T parameters in total and 49B active per token | All experts must still be loaded, so memory stays large while compute per token stays small | Efficient frontier-scale models (DeepSeek V4, Qwen3.8-Max, Mistral Large 3) |
| Reasoning | The model spends extra computation working through the problem before the answer | Higher latency and cost per answer; effort is adjustable on the 2026 flagships | Mathematics, complex coding, planning, multi-step analysis, agent workflows |
| Decision | The model scores a fixed set of options in one pass and returns a probability per option | Milliseconds, no output tokens | Classification, routing, prioritisation, grading (Microsoft Decision-1) |

The categories overlap: DeepSeek-V4-Pro is a mixture of experts and a reasoning model, and Decision-1 is post-trained from a dense 9B Qwen model.

## How do you route between models in production?

Do not hard-code one model into every feature. A routing layer selects the model per task, and the task type is only the first signal. Mine looks like this in its simplest form:

```python
class ModelRouter:
    """Picks the model per task; the strings are deployment names in Foundry or vLLM."""

    def select_model(self, task: str) -> str:
        if task == "classification":
            return "microsoft-decision-1"
        if task == "complex_reasoning":
            return "gpt-6-sol"
        if task == "coding":
            return "claude-sonnet-5-5"
        if task == "multimodal":
            return "gemini-4-argon"
        if task == "private_inference":
            return "qwen3.8-27b"
        if task == "social_intelligence":
            return "grok-4.7"
        if task == "cost_efficient_reasoning":
            return "deepseek-v4-flash"
        return "gpt-6.1-terra"
```

In production the router weighs more than the task type:

1. **Expected quality** on that task, from your own evaluation set, not a leaderboard.
2. **Price** per million tokens, input and output, including cached-input rates where the vendor publishes one.
3. **Token counts** of the request: a 300K-token prompt rules out Grok's standard rate and Decision-1's 32K input limit.
4. **Latency** requirements: a decision model answers in milliseconds, a flagship reasoning model in seconds.
5. **Context window** and whether the usable window is as long as the advertised one.
6. **Data sensitivity**: private fields go to the self-hosted route; the rest may use an API under the agreed terms.
7. **Availability** of each provider, with a fallback model and a retry policy.
8. **Retirement dates** of each model name, so a shutdown becomes a one-line change.

## Example: a multi-model invoice pipeline

An accounting application that receives 10,000 invoices a month does not need a flagship for every step. The benefit of a multi-model design is that expensive tokens go only where they earn their keep, and arithmetic goes to Python.

| Stage | Model or tool | Responsibility |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Document ingestion | Azure AI Document Intelligence or Google Document AI | OCR and structured extraction |
| Document understanding | Gemini 4 Argon or GPT-6.1 Terra | Unusual invoice layouts the extraction service missed |
| Data validation | Python and SQL | Amounts, VAT and invoice numbers, deterministically |
| Classification and prioritisation | Microsoft Decision-1 | Which exceptions need a person, in what order |
| Complex financial reasoning | Claude Opus 5.5 or GPT-6 Sol | Investigating unusual transactions |
| Private processing | Qwen3.8-27B or DeepSeek-V4-Flash on vLLM | Sensitive fields that must not leave the network |
| Final approval | A person, or a controlled workflow | Any financial decision that matters |

A model is deprecated when the vendor announces an end date and still answers; it is retired on the day every call fails. ## Which model should you choose?

Figure 3 is the decision flow I walk through with clients. Four questions cover most cases; the footnote covers the rest.

<Figure src="llm-decision.en.svg" caption="Figure 3. Which model first. Fixed answers go to a decision model; data that cannot leave your hardware goes to open weights; images, video and huge document sets go to Gemini; long agentic coding and hard reasoning go to a flagship; everything else starts on the mid tier." />

When not to switch:

- **A new model is rarely a reason to switch.** The successor gives different answers on your documents even when it is better; rerun the evaluation set before moving.
- **Do not write "always the latest model" into a policy.** The flagship of September 2026 costs eight times more per million input tokens than the flagship of a year earlier.
- **Do not pay for reasoning on grounded summarising.** Reasoning helps on open problems and can hurt on tasks where the answer is in the retrieved passage; only your own test tells you which case you are in.
- **Do not send a fixed-option decision to a flagship.** A decision model or a small open model does it in milliseconds for cents.

The practical project that teaches all of it: a multi-model enterprise platform with Microsoft Foundry as the model management layer, GPT, Claude and Gemini as external providers, Qwen and DeepSeek on vLLM, Microsoft Decision-1 for routing and classification, LangGraph for orchestration, PostgreSQL with pgvector for retrieval, Redis for caching, FastAPI for the API, Docker and Kubernetes for deployment, and MLflow for evaluation. That is an enterprise architecture, not another chatbot.
