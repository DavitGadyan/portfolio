## The problem

Most discretionary traders do not lose because their plan is bad. They lose because, under pressure, they stop following it: an entry taken before the confirmation, a stop moved, a session that drifts away from the rules written down calmly the night before. The client trades a documented method of his own and wanted what a good independent consultant provides: someone who knows the plan cold, looks at the same chart, and says "this is not what your plan says", every time, without getting tired or emotional.

He also wanted the opposite of a generic trading chatbot. In his words, the assistant had to understand a proprietary trading methodology, interpret complex market structures, integrate with external charting tools, keep contextual awareness across trading sessions and support nuanced, discretionary decision-making. All of that is covered by an NDA, so this page describes the engineering, not the method.

## What I built

- **Strategy-first configuration.** The method is not hard-coded. It is loaded as a versioned package: ordered steps, named rules with their checks, and the trader's own reference documents, chunked and embedded so the assistant can retrieve them. Steps can be reordered and rules edited in the app, and each change is published as a new version, so the strategy evolves without a developer.
- **Answers that cannot drift.** Every reply is structured: the decision, the reasons, the rules applied, the evidence used, the uncertainty and the next step. The assistant works inside the current step only and cites only the evidence it was shown; the code strips any claim that is not backed by evidence before the trader sees it, and a second model critiques each answer first.
- **A review loop with a gate.** The trader can approve, challenge or correct any reply, and a correction changes the session state immediately. Approved feedback is frozen into a dataset and turned into a candidate learning state: worked examples and guidance, not fine-tuning. An independent judge model scores the candidate on a frozen holdout set across ten dimensions, four of them hard gates. It is promoted only if it passes without regressing against the live version, and it can be rolled back.
- **Sessions with memory.** Live and backtest sessions keep their state, messages, observations and market snapshots; each turn sees the recent conversation, the session state and the documents most relevant to the current step. Live sessions read the chart from the trader's charting tool; backtests run on CSV files, a market-data library or synthetic series.
- **Built to run anywhere.** FastAPI and Postgres with pgvector behind a Next.js front end with streaming replies and lightweight charts. Any OpenAI-compatible endpoint can serve the primary, critique and judge roles, so models can be swapped or self-hosted, with no-training and zero-retention settings where the provider offers them. A mock model and synthetic market data let the whole system run offline, and 52 backend and 29 frontend tests cover the step state machine, the API flow, the evaluation scorer and the data layer.
